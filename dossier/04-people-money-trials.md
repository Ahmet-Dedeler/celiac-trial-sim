# People, Money, and Open Trials

## The labs that matter

If you want to be useful, attach to one of these rather than working alone.

| Who | Where | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| **Ludvig M. Sollid** | Univ. of Oslo (K.G. Jebsen Coeliac Disease Research Centre) | The intellectual center of the field. HLA-DQ2/DQ8 restriction, the epitope nomenclature, and the decades-persistence clonotype work. Any epitope-repertoire project should be validated against his data |
| **Bana Jabri / Valérie Abadie** | Univ. of Chicago | IL-15 biology and the **only mouse that develops real villous atrophy** (DQ8-Dd-villin-IL-15tg). Gatekeepers of the preclinical bottleneck |
| **Chaitan Khosla** | Stanford | Glutenase and TG2 chemistry; originated much of the enzyme-therapy line |
| **Detlef Schuppan** | Mainz / Harvard | TG2 inhibitor lineage that became ZED1227/TAK-227 |
| **Robert P. Anderson** | (ex-ImmusanT), Australia/US | The IL-2 release assay and gluten-challenge pharmacodynamics. Also ran Nexvax2 — i.e. knows exactly how tolerance fails |
| **Knut E. A. Lundin** | Oslo | Clinical trials, refractory disease |
| **Elena Verdú** | McMaster | Microbiome, gluten-degrading bacteria, gnotobiotic models |
| **Joseph Murray** | Mayo Clinic | US trial infrastructure and epidemiology |
| **Francisco Barro** | CSIC Córdoba, Spain | The CRISPR low-gliadin wheat program |

Cold outreach works better here than in most fields — it's a small, underfunded community and
they are used to being ignored. Lead with a finished artifact, not with a question.

## Money

- **Celiac Disease Foundation Impact Fund** — launched 18 Mar 2026, phase-one target **$15M**,
  run with **Triple G Ventures** and an independent investment committee. Pillars: early
  detection, prevention, education, cures. Actively sourcing capital *and* evaluating companies.
  This is the most direct door for either investing or being invested in.
- **NIAID celiac NOSI** — NIH has a Notice of Special Interest for celiac research. Relevant if
  you partner with an academic PI.
- **Beyond Celiac** — funds research grants, runs `Go Beyond Celiac` patient registry.
- Baseline for scale: NIH spends roughly **$9M/year** on celiac. A $25–50M focused effort would
  be several times the entire national research budget for the disease.

## For someone who has had celiac for 20 years

Not medical advice — things worth raising with their gastroenterologist:

- **Confirm mucosal healing.** A meaningful fraction of long-diagnosed adults on a strict diet
  still have persistent villous atrophy, often silently. After 20 years this is worth knowing,
  both for their health and because it determines trial eligibility (several trials specifically
  enroll non-responsive patients with residual damage).
- **Objective adherence testing exists now** — urine/stool gluten immunogenic peptides detect
  real exposure that symptom diaries miss.
- **Trial eligibility varies sharply by arm.** Tolerance and TG2-inhibitor trials generally want
  well-controlled patients who can do a gluten challenge; anti-IL-15/CD122 trials often want
  non-responders. Different people qualify for different things.
- The near-term realistic win is **protection from cross-contamination**, not freedom to eat
  bread. Setting that expectation matters.
